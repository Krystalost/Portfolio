The Neighbourhood - Sweater Weather

The 2012 indie-rock song that became the internet's favorite autumn anthem.


Quick Facts

Artist: The Neighbourhood
Album: I Love You.
Release Date: March 28, 2012 (Single) / April 23, 2013 (Album)
Genre: Indie Rock / Alternative Pop
Length: 4:00
Producers: Justyn Pilbrow, Emile Haynie

About the Song

"Sweater Weather" was written and recorded by the California rock band The Neighbourhood in 2012. At the time, the five band members were still in their early twenties and unknown to the public. They uploaded the song to the internet, and it quickly caught the attention of Columbia Records. It was later included on their first studio album, I Love You., in 2013 and became the band's signature hit.

Music and Sound

The song mixes two different music styles: indie rock and hip-hop. It starts with a calm, echoing electric guitar melody that gives a cool beach atmosphere. Then, a heavy hip-hop drum beat begins. Lead singer Jesse Rutherford sings the verses very fast, almost like a rap song, before slowing down for a loud and melodic rock chorus. In the last minute of the song, the tempo slows down completely for an emotional finish.

Song Meaning

The title "Sweater Weather" refers to the cool autumn months when people start wearing warm clothes. In the lyrics, cold weather is used as an excuse for two people to get closer and keep each other warm. The song talks about young love, driving along the California coast, holding hands inside jacket pockets, and feeling safe together when the weather outside is cold and rainy.

Records and Success

"Sweater Weather" is famous for becoming popular twice in two different decades:
2013 Radio Hit: When it first came out in 2013, it reached #1 on the US Billboard Alternative Songs chart and stayed there for 11 weeks.
2020s TikTok Comeback: Nearly ten years after its release, the song went viral on TikTok and Instagram. Every year when October and November arrive, millions of users share autumn videos using this song.
Streaming Numbers: Thanks to social media, the song passed 3 billion streams on Spotify, making it one of the most-streamed rock songs of all time.

Music Video

The music video was filmed entirely in black and white. When the band started their career, they had a strict rule: all of their videos, photos, album covers, and stage lights had to be black and white. In the video, singer Jesse Rutherford and the band members drive an old convertible car, hang out at a retro motel, and perform the song together. This simple, colorless style helped give the song a timeless look.
